## Assessment

The broad ordering is mostly defensible, but I would **not certify every quote as
accurate**. Drake is the clearest magnitude concern. Several emerging artists
have growth premiums that need stronger corroboration, and reception evidence is
not broad enough to establish what all fans think. There is also concrete evidence
of incorrect article identity and release classification.

These are fantasy-market units without a traded external dollar benchmark. Artist
stature, catalogue depth, current consumption, audience growth, video reach,
verified events and reception inform the judgments below. Monthly listeners are
unique listeners in a rolling window, not stream counts, revenue or concert sales.
A quote need not change by the same percentage as listeners. My judgments concern
relative placement, direction and evidence strength; I have not invented exact
"correct" dollar targets or rewritten historical quotes.

## Concrete defects corrected in this review

1. A September 19 **Nick Drake / Lizzy McAlpine** article was saved under rapper
   Drake with the synthesized title "Drake - Plagiarism in music is always a hot
   topic". The article is about different musicians. Verified publisher headlines
   now take precedence over synthesized event titles, and full-name homonyms
   cannot supply a shorter Drake alias. Separate genuine Drake mentions survive.
   This applies to collection checks, stored-event signal eligibility and news
   artist badges. It is not claimed to explain Drake's entire rally; this old
   article was absent from the latest scored event list.
   [Original publisher article](https://stereogum.com/2511966/lizzy-mcalpine-got-stella-leftyd-over-nick-drake-soundalike-on-her-new-album/news).
2. Explicitly disappointing Billboard/chart debuts are classified as commercial
   decline evidence instead of positive album announcements. A stale stored
   positive classification is withheld when rescored; fresh collection supplies
   the corrected classification. This is bounded to explicit commercial wording,
   not a blanket rule that any negative word should crash an artist.
3. A new song/video ahead of or from a project receives single/video treatment,
   including stored events with obsolete album metadata. A real album release
   remains eligible for project-release weighting. Regression cases cover the
   observed Doechii and Quavo errors.

These corrections are model **rmi-core-v44**. They do not set target returns,
reset prices, delete original quotes or fabricate daily graph variation. They also
**do not prove the remaining magnitude calibration is solved**.

## Evidence limitations and model follow-up

- All 89 artists have fresh October 18 Spotify, Last.fm and YouTube audience
  observations in the captured run. That establishes collection coverage, not
  complete sentiment coverage or an independently correct dollar valuation.
- 40/89 YouTube reception samples are insufficient; another 10 are baseline-only.
  69/89 Bluesky sources are baseline-only. Unknown sentiment must not become
  positive sentiment. A positive artist-owned-channel sample can disagree with
  wider criticism without either being representative of every listener.
- Apple song feeds are present in the captured payload, but US and Canadian album
  feeds are missing. Safeguards withhold incomplete album momentum; this is missing
  evidence, not proof that an album has zero demand. Saved provider errors remain
  available for diagnosis.
- Source names are not independent corroboration when one publisher repeats a
  chart result or a social account links the same article. Distinct chart outcomes
  can be real, but cumulative daily attention needs testing separately from new
  information. Drake's headlines also include a financed K-Os project: financing
  another artist's album must not become a solo Drake release bonus.
- The shared listing formula compresses logarithmic audience metrics into roughly
  $8–$140 opening quotes. Its output is an internal listing anchor, not external
  fair value; mature quotes can rise above it. Matching every artist to that
  formula would be circular validation and would erase legitimate momentum.
- Before claiming improved valuation accuracy, evaluate whether forward price
  moves predict independent later demand across the whole roster and release/non-
  release windows. Compare a measured-demand baseline with event, reception and
  technical additions using time-separated evaluation. Test one chart story's
  total impact across daily reruns and repeated publisher coverage. Do not pick
  coefficients merely to make Kanye rise or make all lines look busy.

This review uses a frozen production capture of 89 artist rows, 7,523 price-history
rows, 356 signal snapshots, 24,631 selected source observations and 452 saved event
rows. Saved article lists are discovery material; the independently opened sources
support the specific event comparisons above. For quieter artists, absence of a
verified current catalyst in this review means **not established**, not "nothing
happened". No provider subscription, Reddit collection or price/history reset was
performed.
